3. Faithfulness to Cauchy's theorem hypothesis

`CauchyRigidityCertificate` does NOT faithfully encode the full hypothesis of Cauchy's rigidity theorem: there is no convex polyhedron type, no pair of embeddings, no face congruence/isometry data, no edge-length-preserving flex, no noncongruence hypothesis, and no theorem deriving `edgeSigns` from actual dihedral-angle differences of two convex realizations.

What it contains is an abstract contradiction package:
- `edgeSigns : Fin E → EdgeSign`, with nontriviality at Chapter13.lean:408-409.
- abstract per-vertex arm data at Chapter13.lean:410-411.

4. Verdict on vacuity / genuineness

As a Lean implication over `cert`, `chapter13` and `chapter13_rigidity` are vacuous in the ordinary logical sense because `CauchyRigidityCertificate.isEmpty` proves the premise type is empty for any edgeSigns.

As a mathematical component, it is best classified as COMBINATORIAL-CORE-ONLY, not a faithful unconditional Cauchy rigidity theorem. The certificate is an abstract sign-counting plus arm-obstruction package. `cert → False` proves that such a complete abstract counterexample package is inconsistent. It does not by itself prove that a nontrivial isometric deformation/flex of a convex polyhedron would produce such a certificate.

So: not GENUINE as stated for real convex polyhedra. Also not merely the all-zero/empty-edge triviality: the universal emptiness uses the finite counting contradiction in `chapter13`. The faithfulness gap is the absent flex/polyhedron-to-certificate construction.

5. Exact gap to a faithful unconditional Cauchy rigidity theorem

Need a theorem constructing the relevant certificate or, better, a faithful replacement structure from actual geometry:

- A formal convex polyhedron / convex polytope boundary type with vertices, edges, faces, incidence, triangulation or reduction to triangulated faces, and Euler/face-edge counting supplied as theorems, not fields.
- Two realizations/embeddings of the same combinatorial polyhedron in Euclidean 3-space.
- Face congruence / isometric embedding data proving corresponding face edge lengths agree.
- Dihedral angle definitions on actual polyhedron edges, and `edgeSigns` defined as the sign of the actual dihedral-angle difference.
- Nontrivial flex/deformation hypothesis: distinct/noncongruent realization with at least one nonzero dihedral-sign change, or a proof that if all signs are zero then the realizations are congruent.
- Vertex-link bridge: around each active vertex, the real spherical link order agrees with the combinatorial cyclic order, equal face data gives equal spherical side lengths and fixed closing chord, and low sign-change cases produce the `CauchyArmFixedChordObstruction` used by `CauchyArmVertex`.
- Count bridge: face signs and vertex sign changes must be derived from one actual incidence structure. In current `CauchyRigidityCertificate`, `faceSigns`, `vertexArmData`, Euler, and `3F=2E` are independent fields.

Outside the requested Chapter13 target, `Ch13CauchyAssembly.lean` explicitly identifies the old certificate as unfaithful at lines 8-10 and sketches a replacement split; `Ch13Realization.lean` defines a conditional `ConvexPolytopeRealization` interface at lines 316-367 and proves `realization_rigid` at lines 534-543. But its own header says it is not a full ℝ³ polytope model and remains conditional on a realization datum, Ch13Realization.lean:16-18 and 34-39. This confirms the Chapter13 theorem itself is not the unconditional Cauchy theorem.
